The philosophy

The right brain isn't a luxury.
It's how you survive.

After 10+ years placing executives across global pharmaceutical and biotech companies — over 10,000 interviews, €14M in revenue — I noticed a pattern.

The most sustainably effective leaders weren't just analytically brilliant. They had a creative practice. A physical discipline. A way of staying connected to themselves underneath the pressure.

The ones who didn't? They burned out. Or they succeeded in ways that slowly hollowed them out. Most people ignore the signals until they crash. Then it's 6–12 months of forced recovery — the career cost and opportunity cost of which is immense.

We've been optimised into left-brain-only existence. Constant stimulation, dopamine hits masquerading as fulfilment, jobs that consume us. The antidote isn't quitting everything. It's reawakening the right brain — through creativity, movement, and the body's own intelligence — before you're forced to stop.

The Kennedy Wiggle

Are you in an upward or downward spiral? Good feelings breed more good feelings. Bad feelings breed more bad feelings. The Kennedy Wiggle is how you work with that — not against it. It runs through everything I do.

01
Acknowledge

Where are you right now — upward spiral or downward? Just notice.

02
Bring it closer

Sit with it. Let it inform you. Don't push it away.

03
Do something with it

Move your body. Make something. Create.

The Burnout
Prevention Programme

This isn't for people who are already on the floor. It's for people who can feel it coming — the Sunday dread, the Thursday pit-of-stomach feeling, the sense that something needs to change before it changes you.

Most people ignore the signals until they crash. Then it's 6–12 months of forced recovery. The career cost and the opportunity cost of that is immense.

"The goal is to choose your path — not to be forced into it by a breakdown."

Over nine weeks, we use the Kennedy Wiggle, colour mapping, body-based decision-making, and daily creative practice to build a different relationship with yourself and your work. Not art — creativity. Walking a different route. Drawing with your non-dominant hand. Noticing what your body already knows.

The Kennedy Wiggle runs throughout every session. We also work with the Body Compass — a practice for learning to read your own yes and no when analytical thinking alone won't get you there.

This is not therapy. Not psychology. Not for people who don't trust their intuition or won't trust a process. It's for leaders who are ready to use a different kind of intelligence.

Why Your Gut Matters More Than Ever (And How to Actually Use It)

In a time of AI and endless data, how important is your gut feeling? After 10,000+ hours interviewing executives, I've seen that the best leaders don't just rely on data. They've learned to listen to their bodies.

Read more →

I've spent the last decade interviewing executives for over 10,000 hours. A clear trend I saw was that the best leaders aren't just analytical powerhouses — they're the ones who've figured out how to access something deeper. Something that doesn't live in spreadsheets or strategy decks.

They've learned to listen to their bodies.

The science behind your gut feel

Harvard Business Review research shows that people who combine data and intuition outperform those relying on analytics alone. Antonio Damasio's research on the Somatic Marker Hypothesis demonstrates how physical sensations guide decision-making in complex situations. Laura Huang at Harvard Business School confirms that gut feelings are essential when more data won't give you greater clarity.

How the best leaders use it

Recently, I asked a long-standing client how playing the guitar has impacted his job. His answer: it "makes me a better me and better leader. It is my therapy, ventilation and energy booster." The left brain — strategic, analytical — gets you in the room. The right brain — creative, intuitive, body-connected — is what makes you sustainably effective.

Simple ways to start

Recognise your yes. What does agreement feel like in your body? Lightness? Expansion?

Recognise your no. What does resistance feel like? Heaviness? Contraction?

Walk a different route. Turn right when you'd normally turn left. Simple and creative.

Find your guitar or your clay. What makes you lose track of time? That's your doorway to the right brain.
